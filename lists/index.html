How We Find Gwinnett Homeowners Who Are Ready to Sell (and Prove It)

Most lead lists are a pile of names. Ours starts with the one public record that shows real financial pressure: unpaid property taxes.

1. Start with the public record

Gwinnett County publishes a weekly delinquent-tax export. The latest one has 278,302 rows. That sounds like a goldmine until you open it: 89% of the rows are current-year bills that hadn't come due yet.

2. Strip out the noise

We keep only real property with back taxes from prior years. Business equipment accounts go. So do government and church parcels, and the big rental funds that own hundreds of homes and will never be a motivated seller. What's left is about 3,500 parcels.

3. Read the signals

Years owed matters most: an owner three or more years behind is under real pressure. Next is whether the owner lives there. If the tax bill goes to a different address, that's an absentee owner, often a landlord or an heir. If it goes to the house, that's an owner-occupant in a different kind of trouble. We label every lead so you know which conversation you're walking into.

4. Find the person

A tax record gives a name and a mailing address. We run each owner through a skip trace to find current phone numbers.

5. Check our own work

This is the step most list sellers skip. We compare the person we found to the tax record. If the name and current address both match, the record is marked confirmed. If the name matches but the address doesn't, it's probable. Anything else is flagged. In our first test batch, about half of the traced owners were confirmed. We tell you that, because you'll be calling these people and you deserve a straight number.
