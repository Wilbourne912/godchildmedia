Introducing: Vincent Wilbourne

No laptop. No office. No big team. 📱

Every page you've seen on this site was built from an Android phone.

I'm Vincent Wilbourne, lead developer at God Child Media. I'm self-taught, I build with a terminal app called Termux and a browser, and I test everything locally before it goes live. I'd rather show you something that works than tell you something that sounds good.

God Child Media exists to provide AI-powered solutions for content creation, small business growth and brand development. Here's what that looks like in practice. 👇

🛠️ What I've built

🏛️ A civic engagement platform for a Georgia political action committee. Members sign in with Google, join forums, search real legislation, look up their officials by ZIP code and file complaints through a guided form with built-in rate limiting. Behind it: an admin dashboard, role-based access, a blog and a database I designed from scratch. I took it on at a steep discount because I wanted proof, not a portfolio of tutorials.

🍽️ A restaurant demo site with a menu, reservations, online ordering, an owner dashboard and a guided tour that doubles as a sales pitch. The demo cleans itself every hour, and sign-ups are locked down so nobody can wreck it.

🌌 This site. Cards, nav links, hero images and posts are all editable from an admin portal, no code changes needed. Visitors can read and sign up, but only an admin can change anything. That's the setup I'd build for a small business owner: a site you can run yourself, with no monthly software fee.

📋 Contractor lead lists, scraped from public sources, cleaned, formatted into spreadsheets and delivered with a cold-calling guide.

I'm building two original characters, each with their own bio, sign-up page and transmissions feed: a faceless artist and an AI influencer named Valorie The Sylph.

I'm also building an AI-assisted pipeline that turns a script into a finished short video. It isn't magic. I read every script and judge every video before anything gets posted.

💰 The online money research

I spend a lot of time asking where the web will actually pay people. Right now I'm exploring three ideas.

🗺️ A local business index. I'm starting with Savannah restaurants and food trucks, pulling scattered public records like health inspection scores and business registrations into one place. Listings are verified before they're published, and every page carries a disclaimer, because both people and AI make mistakes.

🔍 Local search. Pages that answer real local questions can rank on their own, with no ads and no cold calls.

🤖 Data for AI. Cloudflare has already launched a pay-per-crawl program, which hints that structured data could one day be worth paying for. I'm watching it closely. I'm not betting my rent on it.

⚡ How I actually work

I'm direct and I move fast. I'd rather hear "this is broken" on day one than polite praise on day thirty, and I expect the same honesty from the people and the AI tools I work with.

My build process has a clear split: Gemini handles layout, Claude writes the database and the code logic, and I test and decide.

I learn by building and by getting quizzed, not by watching lectures. Right now I'm working toward junior DevOps, starting with Postgres.

I also change direction quickly. I started with web development, found cold outreach crowded, and shifted toward content and audience building while keeping the web skills. If you want someone who never pivots, that's not me. If you want a builder who tests, measures and adapts, that is.
