Spent A Quarter ( Ai Recasting)

How We Put an AI Character Into a Viral Dance in One Take

If you've scrolled YouTube or TikTok lately, you've heard Quarters, the new snippet from Lil Uzi Vert. Right next to it is his dance video, where he hits the new bend dance. It's everywhere. 🎶

It also landed on the For You page of one very specific fan: Valorie.

Valorie is our AI character, and she's a Uzi fan. So she didn't just watch the trend. She jumped in. 💃

🎯 The Idea

We already had a set of images we use to train Valorie's LoRA, which keeps her face and look consistent. We took those images to fal.ai and ran them through MiniMax's Recast model to place her into the dance.

🚀 The Result

One take. The model handled the lighting, the camera cuts and the overall quality without us patching anything afterward. That almost never happens on the first try.

🧠 Why It Worked

The model did great, but we didn't leave it to chance. Video recasting works best when the input is easy to read. So before generating anything, we edited the original clips to make them as digestible as possible: trimmed to the moments that matter, kept the framing clean, and cut anything that could confuse the model.

Cleaner input, cleaner output. That prep is most of the craft, and it's exactly what we bring to a client project.

🛠️ The Tool Stack

Here's what we used, what it solves, and what it cost.

🎨 Google Flow (images)
Generates and refines the character images. It solves the first problem in any AI video job: you need consistent, high-quality reference art before you start.

🎥 MiniMax Video via fal.ai
Swaps a character into existing footage while keeping the motion, lighting and camera work. It solves the cost of re-shooting or hand-animating a dance, because the motion that already works gets reused. fal.ai hosts the model, so there's no GPU to buy or maintain. Cost for the full recast generation: $1.53.

✂️ CapCut
Our editor. We use it to trim and prep the source clips, then finish the final cut. Messy footage gives messy AI output, so clean prep makes the model's job easy.

🗜️ FFmpeg
Compresses the final video from the terminal. Big files upload slowly and get crushed by platform compression. FFmpeg shrinks them while protecting quality. It's free.

📅 Buffer
Schedules the post. Trends have a window, and scheduling lets us hit the right time without being glued to the phone.

⚡ The Takeaway

None of these tools is exotic. The value is in the order: strong reference images, prepped footage, one smart generation, tight compression, timed posting. Done right, you go from "that trend is hot" to "our character is in it" fast.
